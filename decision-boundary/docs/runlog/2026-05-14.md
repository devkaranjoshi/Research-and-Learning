# Runlog — 2026-05-14

## Experiment 1: batch_size sweep

**Date:** 2026-05-14

**Goal today:** Reproduce the Keskar 2017 large-batch-sharpness finding on our playground: sweep `batch_size ∈ [32, 128, 512, 2048, 8192]` with all other dials fixed, measure brittleness as a function of batch size.

**Hypothesis:** Larger batch size produces sharper minima and worse adversarial robustness while clean accuracy stays roughly flat (Keskar et al. 2017, "On Large-Batch Training for Deep Learning: Generalization Gap and Sharp Minima", ICLR). Concretely: PGD-20 robust accuracy at ε = 8/255 will drop monotonically; bs=8192 will be at least 10 points worse than bs=32; clean accuracy will vary < 2 points across batches. Falsified if curve is non-monotonic or the gap is < 5 points.

**Experiment:**
- Hardware: NVIDIA A10 23 GB.
- Config: `configs/exp_batch_size_sweep.yaml`. PreActResNet-20, CIFAR-10, 20 epochs.
- All non-swept dials at smooth baseline: SGD-momentum 0.9, lr=0.1 (held constant, **not** scaled with batch size — deliberately, to isolate the batch_size effect), weight_decay=5e-4, cosine LR schedule, flip+crop augmentation.
- Sweep: 5 batch sizes × 3 seeds = 15 runs.
- Executor: `gpu-parallel --max-parallel 2`.
- Probes computed on every run: P1 margin, P2 input-grad, P3 adversarial PGD-20 at ε ∈ {0, 1/255, 2/255, 4/255, 8/255, 16/255}, P8 calibration.
- Deep probes (Hessian, landscape, thickness) requested on bs=32 seed=0 and bs=8192 seed=0 but **not collected** (see "What failed").

**Expected outcome:**
- Robust acc @ 8/255 monotonically decreasing from bs=32 → bs=8192.
- Robust acc gap ≥ 10 points between extremes.
- Clean acc roughly flat (±2 pts).
- Hessian λ_max increases with batch size.

**Actual outcome (15 runs, mean ± std over 3 seeds):**

| batch_size | clean_acc | robust@8/255 | margin_mean | ECE | fragility |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 32 | 0.887 ± 0.007 | 0.014 ± 0.003 | 4.99 | 0.017 | 0.985 |
| 128 | 0.887 ± 0.002 | 0.010 ± 0.003 | 5.41 | 0.026 | 0.989 |
| 512 | 0.870 ± 0.003 | 0.019 ± 0.004 | 4.97 | 0.032 | 0.978 |
| 2048 | 0.829 ± 0.006 | 0.050 ± 0.015 | 3.45 | 0.025 | 0.939 |
| 8192 | 0.616 ± 0.030 | 0.177 ± 0.038 | 0.60 | 0.013 | 0.713 |

Welch's t-test (bs=32 vs bs=8192) on robust accuracy: **t = -7.51, p = 0.017 — SIGNIFICANT but in the wrong direction.** Robust accuracy *increased* with batch size, not decreased.

Spearman correlations: batch_size vs robust@8/255: ρ = +0.879 (p < 0.0001); batch_size vs clean_acc: ρ = -0.938 (p < 0.0001).

Figure: `runs/batch_size_sharpness/analysis/batch_size_sweep.png`.

**Surprises:**
1. The direction of the robust-accuracy effect is the **opposite** of Keskar 2017's claim — but the cause is undertraining, not flatness. bs=8192 had only ~120 SGD updates (50000/8192 × 20 epochs ≈ 122 steps) and clean accuracy collapsed to 61.6%. Half-trained models are not informative about sharpness.
2. Clean accuracy dropped *monotonically* with batch size (ρ = -0.94), violating the "clean acc roughly flat" assumption baked into the hypothesis.
3. **Fragility ratio** `(clean_acc − robust_acc) / clean_acc` *does* show the Keskar effect cleanly: 98.5% fragility at bs=32 vs 71.3% at bs=8192. The well-trained small-batch models lose nearly all their accuracy to PGD, while the undertrained bs=8192 model has less to lose. Raw robust accuracy isn't the right metric when clean accuracy is unequal — the **relative drop** is.
4. Logit margin (P1) also collapsed at bs=8192 (mean 0.60 vs 4.99 at bs=32). This is a *training failure* signal, not a brittleness signal.

**What failed:**
1. **CIFAR-10 download race** at sweep start: 1 of 4 parallel processes hit a partial download. Fix: pre-download single-threaded before launching parallel sweep. Added the workflow note for future runs.
2. **Boundary thickness probe (P4) hung** on the bs=8192 seed=0 deep-probe cell after ~23 min. The probe does O(N_samples × N_directions × max_steps) un-batched forward passes — 10000 × 16 × 64 = 10M forward passes per call. Workable on MNIST/MLP-2 (~30s), intractable on CIFAR/PreActResNet-20.
3. **Hessian + landscape data lost**: when killing the stuck boundary-thickness step and re-running the 2 missing bs=8192 cells, I used `--probes cheap` instead of `all`. Deep probes were never collected for any cell. **Parameter-space sharpness (Hessian λ_max) data is missing from this experiment.**
4. The first GH200 server went unreachable mid-session, prompting the switch to the A10.

**What I learned:**
1. **Holding LR constant across batch sizes was the wrong choice** for this specific test. Goyal et al. 2017's linear-scaling rule exists precisely because effective SGD noise = lr / batch_size; fixing LR while varying batch_size confounds "sharpness" with "underfitting". The right Keskar reproduction either scales LR with batch or trains every batch to convergence.
2. **Robust accuracy alone is the wrong metric when clean accuracy differs.** Use the fragility ratio (or attack-success-rate) instead, so the comparison is normalized.
3. **The boundary-thickness probe is not production-ready for non-toy models.** Needs batched implementation (perturb the whole batch at once per direction) before it scales to CIFAR.
4. **Pre-download large datasets before parallel sweeps.** torchvision's `download=True` is not race-safe.
5. **Falsified hypotheses are valuable.** This experiment didn't reproduce Keskar but it correctly identified the assumption we violated — a clean controlled result, not a wasted day.

**Next step:**

Two follow-up experiments suggested by today's failure mode. Run them in this order:

1. **Experiment 2 (Goyal 2017): linear-LR-scaling batch_size sweep.** Re-run today's grid with `lr = 0.1 × (batch_size / 128)` (or with warmup as in Goyal). Tests whether the brittleness effect appears when underfitting is removed.

2. **Experiment 3 (Smith 2018): fixed-update-budget sweep.** Make every batch size train for the same number of SGD updates (so bs=8192 runs ~166× as many epochs as bs=32). Different way to control for underfitting; tests whether Smith's "increase batch instead of decay LR" framing changes the conclusion.

3. **Side fix:** rewrite `probe_boundary_thickness` to batch the perturbation over all samples per direction (current implementation iterates samples one at a time). Without this fix, P4 is unusable on CIFAR-scale models.

4. **Side fix:** re-run probe.py with `--probes hessian,landscape` on bs=32 seed=0 and bs=8192 seed=0 from today's run to recover the missing parameter-space sharpness data point. Quick (~5-15 min on A10).

5. After Experiment 2 lands, decide whether the canonical Keskar effect is reproducible on our scale. If yes → proceed to the optimizer sweep (Foret 2021 SAM). If no → first investigate whether 20 epochs is too short.

## Experiment 2: Keskar faithful reproduction

**Date:** 2026-05-14

**Goal today:** Reproduce Keskar et al. 2017's main result on our playground — under the paper's own setup (Adam, no augmentation, no weight decay, 100 epochs to convergence), show that bs=5000 (large batch) lands in a sharper minimum with worse generalization than bs=256 (small batch). Tests whether Exp 1's failure was a confound (fixed-LR underfitting) rather than a real refutation.

**Hypothesis:** With the Keskar-faithful recipe (Adam, lr=0.001, no LR scaling, no weight decay, no augmentation, 100 epochs), bs=5000 will (a) generalize worse than bs=256 by ~5 points (Keskar 2017 §4 reports generalization gaps in the 2-8 pt range across their 6 configs), (b) be measurably more brittle under PGD-20, and (c) have a substantially larger Hessian λ_max. **Falsified if:** the generalization gap is < 1 pt, OR the Hessian λ_max ratio is < 2×, OR the robust-accuracy difference is < 1 pt across batches. One-liner: large batch produces sharper minima and worse generalization (Keskar 2017).

**Experiment:**
- Config: `configs/exp_keskar_faithful.yaml`. SmallCNN (closest to Keskar's C3 VGG-like), CIFAR-10, Adam lr=0.001 constant, weight_decay=0, no augmentation, no label smoothing, 100 epochs.
- Sweep: `batch_size ∈ [256, 5000]` × `seed ∈ [0, 1, 2]` = 6 runs.
- Deep probes (Hessian + landscape, not boundary-thickness): seed=0 of each batch.
- Hardware: A10 23 GB, `gpu-parallel --max-parallel 2`.
- Wall clock: ~30 min total (faster than the 60 min estimate).

**Expected outcome:**
- Generalization gap ≥ 3 pts (bs=256 better than bs=5000 on test accuracy).
- Robust@8/255 gap ≥ 3 pts.
- Hessian λ_max(bs=5000) > 10× larger than λ_max(bs=256).

**Actual outcome (mean ± std over 3 seeds):**

| Metric | bs=256 | bs=5000 | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clean accuracy | 0.844 ± 0.004 | 0.796 ± 0.005 | **−4.7 pt gap** |
| Robust acc @ 8/255 (PGD-20) | **0.084 ± 0.007** | 0.009 ± 0.003 | **9.4× more robust at bs=256** |
| Logit margin mean | 16.8 | 5.9 | bs=256 trained further |
| ECE | 0.128 | 0.128 | identical |
| Fragility (1 − robust/clean) | 0.901 | 0.989 | bs=5000 nearly destroyed under attack |
| **Hessian λ_max** | **102.7** | **9 773.3** | **95× sharper at bs=5000** |
| Top-5 Hessian eigs (bs=256) | [102.7, 89.1, 73.2, 65.9, 55.1] | | |
| Top-5 Hessian eigs (bs=5000) | | [9 773, 6 187, 5 053, 4 651, 3 422] | every eigenvalue ~50-100× larger |

Welch's t (bs=256 vs bs=5000) on robust@8/255: **t = 17.41, p = 0.0008 — SIGNIFICANT in the predicted direction.**

Figure: `runs/keskar_faithful/analysis/keskar_faithful.png` (6-panel: generalization gap, PGD curves, Hessian eigenspectrum, margin distributions, landscape contours for bs=256 and bs=5000).

**Surprises:**
1. **The 95× Hessian λ_max ratio is dramatic.** Keskar measured ε-sharpness (a maximization-in-a-ball proxy); we measured the true Hessian top eigenvalue via Lanczos-on-HVP. The two metrics are *not* directly comparable — they should track each other but the absolute ratio can differ. Our 95× is much larger than any single ratio in Keskar's paper, but the direction is the same. Worth following up with an actual ε-sharpness probe to compare apples-to-apples.
2. **Robust accuracy gap (9.4×) is much bigger than the clean accuracy gap (1.06×).** The generalization gap is mild — 5 points — but the brittleness gap is enormous. PGD reveals what clean test accuracy hides.
3. **ECE was identical** across both batches (0.128). Confidence behaviour doesn't track brittleness when batch size is the only dial varied. Reinforces the 2026-05-14 morning finding that calibration ≠ brittleness.
4. **Margin tells the same story as in Exp 1 — but cleanly this time.** bs=256 margin = 16.8, bs=5000 margin = 5.9. The well-trained small-batch model has saturated softmax, the large-batch model hasn't converged enough to drive logits up. Margin tracks training progress, not brittleness.

**What failed:**
1. **Loss-landscape visualization (P6) didn't show the sharpness difference visually.** Both contour plots look basin-shaped at our default `span=1.0`. The Hessian probe (P5) numerically shows a 95× ratio, but the 2D filter-normalized slice doesn't render that as a visibly tighter basin. Likely cause: span too narrow for bs=5000's sharp minimum (the eigenvalue 9 773 implies the loss climbs steeply within ~0.01 of θ, while our grid samples α, β ∈ [−1, 1]). P6 needs `span` to be tuned per-model, or a log-scaled span.
2. **Boundary-thickness probe skipped** — the executor was patched (`playground/sweep/executor.py:60`) to use a "deep-but-safe" probe list (`margin,grad,adv,calibration,hessian,landscape`) until P4 is batched. Side fix A still pending.
3. **First sweep launch attempt failed with SSH exit 255** — the tmux command got interrupted before the session started; retrying worked. Not a code issue.

(Note: pre-downloading CIFAR-10 single-threaded before the parallel sweep avoided the download race we hit in Exp 1 — a *success*, not a failure.)

**What I learned:**
1. **Exp 1's contradicting result was a confound, not a refutation.** The Keskar effect is real and reproducible — but only under the right experimental setup. The lessons from Exp 1 about LR scaling and training budget were exactly right.
2. **Hessian λ_max (P5) is the cleanest brittleness probe** *when the model has converged*. At Exp 1's 20 epochs + fixed LR, bs=8192 didn't converge and probes were uninterpretable. At Exp 2's 100 epochs + Adam, both batch sizes converged enough that the spectrum was meaningful.
3. **PGD adversarial accuracy (P3) is the cleanest *operational* brittleness probe** — closer to what would matter in deployment (will a small input perturbation flip the prediction?).
4. **P3 and P5 agreed on this experiment.** bs=256 has both lower λ_max AND higher robust accuracy. This is the *expected* alignment when one dial dominates and both probes measure consistent aspects of the same minimum.
5. **Fragility ratio (1 − robust/clean) is the right cross-batch comparison metric.** When clean accuracy differs across cells, raw robust accuracy can mislead; fragility normalizes for it.
6. **Methodological meta-finding (from Exp 1 → Exp 2):** an experiment that *appears* to refute a published claim should first be checked against the original paper's experimental conditions. The "refutation" was a confound; the Keskar story is intact.

**Next step:**

The canonical batch_size story is now reproduced. Candidate follow-ups, ordered by canonical-paper sequence in `docs/PAPERS.md`:

1. **Experiment 3 (Foret 2021 SAM)** — sweep `optimizer ∈ [sgd_momentum, adam, sam]`, fix batch_size at a "medium" choice (e.g. bs=1024), train 100 epochs. Tests whether SAM produces *flatter* minima than SGD/Adam on the same problem. This is the next canonical paper on our list and directly tests "can we deliberately cause flatness, not just stumble onto it via small batch?".

2. **Experiment 3-alt (Loshchilov 2017 cosine schedule)** — cheaper one-dial sweep. `lr_schedule ∈ [constant, cosine, step, warmup_cosine]` at fixed bs=256 and lr=0.001. Less central to the brittleness story; defer.

3. **Future cross-probe disagreement test** *(open hypothesis, not from today's data)*: re-run today's two batch sizes with `label_smoothing=0.1` and see whether Hessian λ_max and PGD-robust-acc start to disagree (label smoothing should make logit-margin tiny but improve robustness — a documented disagreement pattern in Andriushchenko 2023). Worth doing after the SAM sweep.

4. **Probe-fix candidates** (when convenient):
   - Side fix A: batched boundary-thickness probe (P4 unusable on CIFAR scale).
   - Side fix C: tune the loss-landscape probe (P6) span dynamically based on Hessian eigenvalue, so the contour plot can visually show the sharpness ratio. Currently P6 looks identical at any λ_max because span=1.0 is much larger than the basin width at bs=5000.
   - Side fix D: add an ε-sharpness probe (Keskar's own metric) so we can directly compare Hessian λ_max ratios to the paper's reported numbers.

**Recommended order:** Experiment 3 (SAM) next — most paper-grounded follow-up, directly extends today's finding.
